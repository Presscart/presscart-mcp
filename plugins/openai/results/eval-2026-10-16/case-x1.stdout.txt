Your plan is worth exploring, with **buyer understanding as the primary goal**. Treat AI visibility and a founder’s O-1 evidence as separate potential benefits, each requiring its own evaluation.

Accepting paid-placement disclosure helps clarify the approach, but disclosure alone doesn’t establish credibility. Evaluate each opportunity on:

| Goal | What would make coverage useful | What to avoid assuming |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer understanding | Relevant readership, a clear product explanation, concrete use cases, supported claims, and meaningful editorial review | A recognizable outlet automatically reaches your buyers |
| AI visibility | Publicly accessible, substantive coverage that clearly identifies your company and product | Publication guarantees inclusion or citations in AI answers |
| O-1 evidence | Coverage with a demonstrable connection to the founder and their work, evaluated by immigration counsel | A paid product placement automatically qualifies as useful immigration evidence |

Before choosing outlets or setting a budget, define the story: **who has what problem, how your product helps, and what evidence supports that claim**. Then distinguish independent editorial coverage, sponsored articles, and syndicated releases when comparing proposals—their value and editorial processes differ.

To qualify the plan further, please share:

- What does the product do, and who buys it?
- What should a buyer understand or do after reading the coverage?
- What proof can you offer—customer examples, results, original data, or founder expertise?
- Is the founder actively preparing an O-1 case, and has counsel identified a specific evidence gap?

Budget, timing, and outlet choices can follow those answers. There’s no need to commit to placements yet.
